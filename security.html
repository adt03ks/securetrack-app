Security & Compliance

SecureTrack OP is designed to support healthcare security operations through authenticated access, role-based workflows, and centralized operational records. We recognize that hospitals require careful security assessment before adopting any new technology.

1. Platform Scope

SecureTrack OP is a security operations management platform intended to assist authorized teams with duty assignments, equipment inspections and checkout, incident alerts and reports, staffing workflows, and property or belongings documentation. Depending on the deployment, records may include employee information, operational details, photographs, device information, or sensitive narratives.

SecureTrack OP is an operational support tool; it does not replace a healthcare organization's clinical record system, emergency policies, or required security controls.

2. Identity & Access Management

The platform uses Supabase-based authentication and is structured around role-specific permissions, including administrator, manager, team lead, senior officer, and officer roles. Workflows are designed to respect management and shift assignments rather than granting all staff unrestricted access.

Access restrictions must be verified through testing for each feature and database operation. Organization-specific controls such as account provisioning, deactivation, periodic access reviews, and multifactor authentication requirements should be evaluated as part of deployment.

3. Data Security & Privacy

SecureTrack OP's current architecture uses Supabase services, including database authorization mechanisms such as Row Level Security (RLS) and controlled database functions. Properly configured policies can help prevent access to records outside a user's authorization.

Encryption in transit, encryption at rest, backup coverage, administrative access restrictions, data isolation, retention rules, secure deletion, and restoration capabilities require confirmation in the specific production configuration and applicable vendor plans. Hospitals should not assume a control is fully implemented solely because the underlying provider offers it.

4. Operational Accountability

Structured workflows support device inspection, checkout and return, equipment recovery reason codes, security incident alerts, property documentation, and duty assignments. These processes are intended to improve traceability and consistency.

Comprehensive, tamper-resistant audit logging—including administrative changes, data access, exports, failures, and record modifications—remains subject to technical validation and should be addressed before enterprise deployment.

5. Notifications & Service Providers

The platform currently incorporates Supabase infrastructure and has been integrating OneSignal for push notifications. Additional hosting or messaging services may be used as the product evolves.

Notification content should minimize sensitive information, directing authorized users to authenticate in the platform for details. Third-party processing, service locations, contractual protections, subprocessors, and vendor retention practices must be reviewed for each healthcare deployment. Push notifications must not be assumed suitable for PHI without a completed review and applicable agreements.

6. Healthcare & HIPAA Considerations

Healthcare security records can sometimes include patient names, photographs, treatment locations, or other information that may constitute PHI. Whether HIPAA applies to a particular implementation depends on the data, the parties' roles, and how the platform is used.

If SecureTrack OP creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a covered entity or business associate, a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) and appropriate HIPAA safeguards may be required. Any applicable upstream or downstream vendor agreements must be assessed before such use. No general statement on this page authorizes customers to enter PHI into the platform.

7. Security Validation & Readiness

Before a hospital uses SecureTrack OP for production operations, a deployment assessment should confirm:

  • Role-based permissions, RLS policies, cross-shift restrictions, and any tenant boundaries.
  • Authentication and session controls, including MFA expectations and account revocation.
  • Secure configuration, patching, secret management, and dependency review.
  • Audit log coverage, record retention, data export, and deletion procedures.
  • Encryption, backup/restore procedures, recovery objectives, and incident-response responsibilities.
  • Notification payload restrictions, vendor contracts, and BAA applicability.
  • Vulnerability assessment and remediation of material findings before go-live.

SecureTrack OP does not currently claim an independently verified SOC 2 report, formal HIPAA attestation, or a particular hospital's production authorization through this page.

8. Healthcare Organization Responsibilities

Each deploying organization should define approved use cases and data classifications, approve user roles, manage access requests and departures, train personnel, assess integrations, and apply its own information security and privacy policies. Hospital IT, legal, privacy, security, and procurement teams should review the solution against their requirements before production use.
